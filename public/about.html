Why I built Vulnder

2025 set a record with 48,185 CVEs.1 Then 2026 doubled the monthly rate, from 5,045 in January to 10,740 in August.2 Most of them don't touch what you run, and only a few of those are being exploited.

Vulnder answers two questions about your stack: which new CVEs affect it, and which of those is anyone exploiting. Describe what you run or drop in a lockfile, and get the answer in seconds, in the order to fix things.

The usual way to triage is running out

For years, "which ones matter?" meant the CVSS score NIST added to each CVE. In April 2026 NIST said it can't keep up, and now scores known-exploited, government and critical software first.3 A year earlier, the CVE programme came within a day of losing its funding.4

A severity score says how bad a bug could be, not whether anyone is exploiting it. And a missing score isn't "safe", so Vulnder says what it doesn't know.

AI cuts both ways

AI is finding serious bugs: half of those in Google's 2026 study lead to remote code execution, against about a quarter of CVEs overall.2

It helps attackers too. Most of 2026's growth in exploitation came from disclosed bugs weaponised quickly, likely with AI reading the patches.2 In the first half of 2026, 23% of newly exploited bugs were exploited on or before the day their CVE was published.5

Why prioritising matters more now

  • Volume has outrun patching. Twice the CVEs doesn't bring twice the people or change windows.
  • Severity no longer sorts. When half of AI-found bugs are remote code execution, "critical" stops telling you where to start.
  • The window has shrunk. Bugs are weaponised within days, sometimes before disclosure.2, 5 A monthly review is too slow.
  • Your fixers are faster too. A coding agent given a ranked list starts where an attacker would, not wherever a raw feed begins.

Evidence beats severity

Evidence of exploitation (CISA's catalog or reports) comes first, then prediction (EPSS, NIST's LEV), then severity (CVSS). Every result says which signal decided its place and what data was missing. Scores only set the order; they aren't probabilities.

What I wanted it to be

  • Fast. A sentence, lockfile or SBOM in, a ranked answer out.
  • No account. Your stack lives in the link, which also gives you a JSON feed, an Atom feed and a badge.
  • Keeps nothing. Your text and stack are never stored or logged, and lockfiles stay in your browser. Cloudflare's AI gateway can log free-text requests. Details
  • Honest. It never hides a match, says how sure each one is, and suggests response times, not deadlines.
  • AI only where it helps. A model reads your description; code does the ranking. Where AI is used
  • Free and open. Apache 2.0, so you can run your own copy.
