About Vulnder

Built by Pete Salmond

The latest CVEs for your stack, aggregated and prioritised. Free and open source.

I built Vulnder because there are now too many CVEs to triage by hand, and most of them don't affect what you run. Too many tools mark everything critical and pass a huge task list downstream, which only adds to the noise.

Tell Vulnder what you run: describe it in a sentence, drop in a lockfile or SBOM, or paste the CVE IDs from a scanner report. It checks them against the latest CVE records, security advisories and exploitation data, then lists the CVEs that affect you, most urgent first, with the reason for each. No account needed.

Why sorting by CVSS no longer works

  • 48,185 CVEs were published in 2025.1 The monthly count doubled during 2026, from 5,045 in January to 10,740 in August.2
  • Since April 2026, NIST has enriched known-exploited, government and critical software first.3 The CVE programme nearly lost its funding in 2025.4
  • Half of the vulnerabilities in Google's 2026 study led to remote code execution, against about a quarter of all CVEs.2
  • 23% of vulnerabilities newly exploited in the first half of 2026 were exploited on or before the day their CVE was published.5

CVSS measures how severe a bug could be. It doesn't say whether anyone is exploiting it, or whether you run the affected software.

In the EU?

NIS2 expects the organisations it covers to monitor and handle vulnerabilities, and the Cyber Resilience Act has manufacturers report actively exploited vulnerabilities in their products. The compliance page sets out what Vulnder does for each point, and what stays with you.

How results are ranked

Evidence of exploitation outranks the predicted probability of exploitation (EPSS, NIST LEV), and both outrank severity (CVSS). Each CVE gets one of four priorities:

PriorityRespondMain triggers
Act nowWithin 24 hours, or 3 days before exploitation is seenIn CISA KEV or actively exploited. Before that: EPSS 50%+, a critical bug with EPSS 10%+ or NIST LEV 20%+, an edge device an attacker can take over automatically, or a critical bug trusted security teams are warning about.
AttendWithin 7 daysEPSS 10%+, NIST LEV 20%+, CVSS 9.0+, a Metasploit exploit module, or a similar CVE in the product exploited.
WatchWithin 30 daysCVSS 8.0+, public proof-of-concept code, or rated automatable by CISA.
TrackNext routine updateAffects your stack; nothing above applies.

Each result lists the signals that set its priority and any data that is missing. A CVE with no score is still shown. EPSS jumps appear under What changed and in the Atom feed. Full ranking rules

Design choices

No accountThe stack is encoded in the URL, so results can be bookmarked or shared. The same link gives an Atom feed, a README badge and, for scripts, a JSON feed.
PrivateOnly free-text entries describing your stack are logged, by Cloudflare's AI Gateway in the account that runs this site. Vulnder doesn't store your stack, your files or your CVE lists; files and CVE lists are read in your browser. Privacy details
Nothing hiddenEvery match is shown. Close matches for broad names are labelled as close.
AI extracts, code ranksA model turns free text into stack items. Code does the matching and ranking. Where AI is used
Open sourceApache 2.0. Self-host it on Cloudflare Workers. Source on GitHub
