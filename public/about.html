About Vulnder

Built by Pete Salmond

I built Vulnder as a free, open-source CVE feed you can use without an account. Describe what you run in a sentence, or drop in a lockfile, SBOM or scanner report, and it lists the CVEs that affect it in the order to fix them, with the reason for each.

Why I built it

In 2025, 48,185 CVEs were published, 20.6% more than in 2024,1 and 2026 is moving faster still.2 Part of that is bookkeeping, now that the Linux kernel assigns its own CVEs.3 Part is AI: Google finds that AI-assisted research turns up more remote code execution bugs, and thinks attackers may be using the same tools to turn patches into exploits.2 Exploitation is faster too. Mandiant puts the mean time to exploit in 2025 at minus seven days,4 VulnCheck found 23% of the CVEs newly exploited in the first half of 2026 were hit on or before the day they were published,5 and NIST now enriches known-exploited and critical software first, with the rest not scheduled for immediate enrichment.6

Most CVEs get a CVSS score from 0 to 10. It rates how much damage a bug could do if someone used it: whether it can be reached over the network, whether it needs a login, how much of the system it hands over. That's a measure of potential. A 9.8 can sit unused for years while a 6.5 is being used in ransomware this week, and the score says nothing about which is which.

Those numbers land on security and systems teams. Nearly 70% of security leaders told HackerOne that findings arrive faster than their teams can fix them,8 scanners hand them every CVE that touches the estate with most of it marked critical, and Verizon puts the median time to fully patch at 43 days, up from 32.7 I've been on those teams, and I built Vulnder because a short list, ranked for the software we ran, would have helped.

Vulnder starts from what you run. Describe your stack and it shows the CVEs that could apply to it, for you to confirm; a broad name like "Cisco switches" brings in close matches, marked as close. Paste the CVE IDs from a scanner report instead and it ranks the ones you already know affect you. Either way, the list is in the order to fix them.

How it ranks CVEs

  • Act now is for CVEs being exploited now or about to be: respond within 24 hours to 3 days.
  • Attend is for those likely to be exploited soon: within 7 days.
  • Watch is serious but less pressing: within 30 days.
  • Track affects your stack, but nothing is urgent: your next routine update.

Evidence of exploitation, such as a listing in CISA KEV, outranks a predicted probability of exploitation (EPSS, NIST LEV), and that outranks severity (CVSS). A model picks product names out of what you type; plain code does the matching and ranking. The full rules are on How it works.

What it keeps

Only free-text entries describing your stack are logged, by Cloudflare's AI Gateway in the account that runs this site. Files and CVE lists are read in your browser, and Vulnder doesn't store them or your stack (privacy details).

Sharing and running your own

Your stack lives in the page link, so results can be bookmarked or shared, and the same link gives an Atom feed, a README badge and a JSON feed. Vulnder is open source under Apache 2.0, and you can run your own copy on Cloudflare Workers (source on GitHub). In the EU, the compliance page sets out what Vulnder does towards NIS2 and the Cyber Resilience Act.

Questions

What is CISA KEV?

The US Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency's catalog of CVEs it has evidence are being exploited. Vulnder puts every CVE on it in Act now, whatever its CVSS.

Is EPSS better than CVSS?

EPSS is a predicted probability that a CVE will be exploited in the next 30 days; CVSS rates how severe it could be. Vulnder ranks EPSS above CVSS, and evidence of exploitation above both.

What does Act now mean?

It's the top priority: respond within 24 hours to a CVE in CISA KEV or reported by CISA as actively exploited, and within 3 days to one that looks about to be, such as a critical bug with a high EPSS.

Does Vulnder scan my systems?

No. It only knows what you give it: a lockfile gives exact versions, and a description gives matches for you to confirm.

Is my stack stored?

Vulnder doesn't store your stack, your files or your CVE lists, and files and CVE lists are read in your browser. Only free-text entries describing your stack are logged, by Cloudflare's AI Gateway in the account that runs this site.
